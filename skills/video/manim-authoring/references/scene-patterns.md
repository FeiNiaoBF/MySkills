# Scene patterns for math-video inserts

## Rate and velocity

Use a position-time graph `s(t)` when the lesson is about motion. Mark two times `t0,t1`, points `(t0,s0),(t1,s1)`, a secant, and coordinate-derived `Δt`/`Δs`. Show `v_avg=Δs/Δt`; distinguish speed with `|v|` instead of treating velocity and speed as synonyms.

## Instantaneous rate

Move `t1` toward `t0`, keep the secant endpoints and increment guides attached to the tracked points, and zoom the local region as the interval shrinks. End with a visible tangent and `v(t0)=ds/dt`; do not rely on a label saying “tangent”.

## Derivative and limit

Show a geometric or algebraic quantity changing before transforming it into a limit expression. Preserve the final derivative expression as a stable anchor for the handoff.

## Continuity and viewpoint

When using a 2D-to-3D explanation, first establish the apparently continuous projection, then rotate or transition to a 3D camera with the hidden coordinate and jump marker visible. Label the projection and the actual path separately so the learner knows which object is continuous.

## Algebraic limit

Keep the transformation chain in one central zone: direct substitution/indeterminate form → factorization → cancellation → limit evaluation. If the formula moves vertically or laterally, keep every state inside the central safe area and inspect the final frame for edge clipping.
