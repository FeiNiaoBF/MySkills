# Writing from the evidence package

Writing begins only after the Research handoff validates. It may improve structure and clarity; it may not invent support, silently upgrade a claim's status, or hide a material disagreement.

## Default expression style

Use clear language inspired by ASD-STE100 as a default, not a compliance gate:

- Put the answer near the start.
- Use direct sentences, active voice where natural, and one main idea per sentence.
- Use the same term for the same concept. Define necessary technical terms once.
- Prefer specific verbs and quantities. Remove repetition and empty emphasis.
- Keep evidence, interpretation, and limitations distinct.
- Preserve quoted text, numbers, negations, conditions, attribution, and uncertainty. Do not shorten away a qualification that changes meaning.
- Adapt to the requested language, audience, and genre. These are plain-language guidelines, not certification against the English standard.

Do not enforce fixed sentence-length limits. If a precise technical sentence must be longer, keep it and make its meaning clear. Use lists or tables when they make dense relationships easier to inspect.

## Keep evidence and synthesis separate

- An **evidence** block reports a claim supported by the recorded ledger. Attach its `claim_ids`.
- A **synthesis** block combines one or more claims. Attach the relevant claim IDs and distinguish the combined interpretation from what a single source states.
- A **context** block gives framing, method, or limitations that are not themselves research claims. Do not use this role to hide uncited factual assertions.

When a new premise is needed, return to Research, add or revise a claim/evidence record, reassess coverage and saturation, then update the article. Do not have Writing fill an evidence gap from model memory.

## Match the requested form honestly

An article or report can synthesize source material. If the user asks for a paper, produce a literature synthesis unless original research was actually performed. Never invent an experiment, result, peer-review status, or methodological procedure.

The page shows the stop reason, important gaps, and limitations. A limited run can still provide a useful answer, but qualify it near the affected conclusion.
