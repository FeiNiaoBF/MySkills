# Research workflow

This reference expands the Research phase in `SKILL.md`. Research ends with an evidence package, not prose polished for readers.

## Frame without over-questioning

Infer an initial brief with:

- the decision or question the user needs answered;
- intended audience and likely output form;
- time sensitivity and useful source preferences;
- the core subquestions that determine whether the answer is adequate;
- the exclusions that materially narrow scope;
- the eight-round default budget, unless the user requests another budget or the task needs a clearly stated limit.

Ask one focused question only when the answer could change the sources, scope, or final conclusion. Otherwise record a reasonable interpretation and start.

## Retrieve and inspect

1. Translate each core question into distinct query formulations and useful search angles.
2. Prefer original research, official records, standards, primary documents, and direct data when the question calls for them. Use secondary sources to orient or explain, not to disguise missing primary evidence.
3. Record the query IDs and actual search/fetch tool names. Keep provider receipt references when available. Search result snippets are discovery leads, not inspected evidence.
4. Fetch and inspect relevant passages. Preserve exact quotations and useful locators, such as section headings, page numbers, timestamps, or stable anchors. Record access failures and unavailable publication dates honestly.
5. Screen fit, evidential support, and usefulness separately. Keep a reason for each selected or excluded source. Deduplicate exact URLs/text; identify syndication and upstream citation origins explicitly.
6. Extract claims from sources, not from the search result page. Attach exact quotations and mark support or contradiction. One origin syndicated by multiple publishers is one evidence origin.
7. Seek important counterpositions and plausible ways the current answer could be wrong. Do not spend rounds repeating queries that returned the same material without a new angle.

## Research records

Use the existing v1 run ledger for queries, sources, claims, conclusions, and evidence graph. Use the report envelope for round-level novelty, assessment, question coverage, and the stop decision. The source/claim ledger is authoritative: do not copy it into separate article graph records.

For each assessed candidate, retain its source record even when it is rejected, redundant, superseded, or unusable. Do not mark retrieval `verified` until the retrieved passage was inspected. A schema-valid package proves structural consistency, not that the source is true or the agent's relevance judgment is correct.

## When tools are missing

If the host has no usable search or page-fetch capability, stop with `retrieval_blocked`. You may offer a clearly labeled background explanation if useful, but do not present it as newly researched or imply evidence saturation.
